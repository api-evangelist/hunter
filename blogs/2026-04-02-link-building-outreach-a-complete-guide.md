<img alt="Link Building Outreach: A Complete Guide" src="https://hunter.io/blog/content/images/2022/12/link-building-outreach.jpg" /><p>SEO is central to Hunter&apos;s success, and in this competitive niche, link building has enabled us to grow and maintain our organic traffic over the years. </p><p>Around 80% of the links we build at Hunter come from relationships&#x2014;not one-off asks. The remaining 20% comes from specific tactics such as link insertions, listicle placements, guest blogging and unlinked-mention outreach. But even those tend to work better when there&apos;s a relationship behind them.</p><p>This guide covers the exact process I use to run link building outreach: how I choose which strategies to prioritize, where I find prospects, how I segment and message to them, and what conversion rates to expect.</p><p>I&apos;ll share email templates for different scenarios, advice on following up, and the lessons that took years of backlink outreach to learn.</p><p>If you&apos;re looking for a broader overview of<a href="https://hunter.io/cold-email-guide/"> <u>cold email outreach</u></a>, we have a separate guide. This one is specifically about building backlinks.</p><h1 id="tldr-link-building-outreach">TL;DR: Link Building Outreach</h1><p>Link building outreach helps grow your website&#x2019;s online visibility by securing backlinks from other relevant domains, using email and other outbound channels to reach them.</p><p>Link building outreach at Hunter is a <strong>relationship-first motion</strong>, not a numbers game. <strong>80% of our links come from partnerships,</strong> with the remaining 20% coming from specific tactics.</p><p><strong>Top strategies (prioritized):</strong></p><ol><li><strong>Editorial links (Link insertions):</strong> The &quot;holy grail.&quot; Insert your link into existing, high-traffic, relevant articles.</li><li><strong>Listicle outreach:</strong> Get placement in &quot;best tools&quot; or &quot;top solutions&quot; articles for bottom-of-funnel traffic and LLM visibility.</li><li><strong>Guest posts:</strong> Build authority and secure writing samples, which lead to higher-tier placements and partner links.</li><li><strong>Unlinked brand mentions:</strong> Pursue opportunistically, but not as a core strategy.</li></ol><p><strong>What we skip:</strong> Broken link building and the Skyscraper technique (low yield, overused).</p><p><strong>The funnel:</strong></p><ul><li><strong>1.3% Prospect-to-Backlink conversion rate</strong> (for every 1,000 prospects emailed, ~13 links are secured).</li><li><strong>66% of replies come from follow-ups.</strong> Always use a 3-email sequence (initial pitch, reframe, breakup).</li></ul><p><strong>Key takeaways for success:</strong></p><ul><li><strong>Relationships are everything:</strong> Focus on offering value (network access, introductions, guest post spots) before asking for a link.</li><li><strong>Segmentation is critical:</strong> Tailor your message based on the recipient&apos;s role (SEO/Content Manager vs. CEO) and the prospect&apos;s DR/domain type.</li><li><strong>Be direct and value-first:</strong> Keep emails short (3&#x2013;5 sentences) and make the offer clear in the subject line or preview.</li><li><strong>Use LinkedIn for compounding benefits:</strong> It bypasses deliverability issues and leverages social proof.</li><li><strong>Don&apos;t obsess over DR (</strong><a href="https://ahrefs.com/blog/domain-rating/"><strong><u>Domain Rating</u></strong></a><strong>):</strong> Prioritize websites that are real businesses with real traffic and genuinely useful content.</li></ul><h2 id="what-is-link-building-outreach"><strong>What is link building outreach?</strong></h2><p>Link building outreach involves using email, LinkedIn, and other outbound channels to acquire external links to your website and online mentions of your business. Both links and brand mentions positively affect your online visibility: not just in Google Search results, but also in LLM responses/answer engines.</p><p>Even though email is a key channel for link builders, link building outreach is closer to <strong>partnership development</strong> than cold emailing&#x2014;even though most guides treat it as the latter.</p><p>The usual framing is transactional: contact website owners, pitch a link, move on. Treat it as a numbers game. Blast enough emails, and some percentage will say yes.</p><p>This approach may work, but it caps your potential.</p><p>The links that actually move rankings&#x2014;<strong>editorial placements on relevant, high-traffic pages</strong>&#x2014;come from people who know you, trust your brand, and see mutual value in working together.</p><p>That&apos;s why I think of outreach link building as two activities running in parallel: relationship building (the long game that compounds over time) and tactical outreach (specific asks for specific link types). Both matter, but the first is what separates sustainable programs from ones that plateau.</p><h2 id="why-link-building-outreach-is-about-relationships"><strong>Why link building outreach is about relationships</strong></h2><p>I maintain active partnerships with 20 to 50 people at any given time. This means we&apos;re in regular contact&#x2014;sharing link opportunities, introducing each other to new partners, collaborating on guest posts. I even ask them for feedback on my sequences and brainstorm solutions when campaigns are underperforming.</p><p>Beyond that, there&apos;s a broader community of roughly 300 people in our partner Slack channel, though not all of them are active.</p><p>This network is the single most valuable asset in our link building program. Here&apos;s why.</p><p>When I reach out to a new prospect, I lead with access to this network. The pitch isn&apos;t &quot;can I get a link?&quot;&#x2014;it&apos;s &quot;we know the challenges of link building, so why don&apos;t we help each other?&quot; I can offer introductions to partners who are relevant to their niche, invite them to a community of SaaS companies doing active link building, or add them to an upcoming guest post.</p><h3 id="how-to-build-your-link-building-network-if-youre-starting-from-zero"><strong>How to build your link-building network if you&apos;re starting from zero</strong></h3><p>If you are new to link building, you likely won&apos;t have a network of link builders (yet).</p><p>The easiest way to build from scratch is to pick a topical niche, write a few guest posts and to look for meaningful relationships with others in your niche. Guest posts connect you with editors and build credibility that helps reach out to others with something specific.</p><p>LinkedIn is the best place to start. Use guest blogging in combination with looking for meaningful partners to create writing samples, build a small portfolio, and lay the foundations of a network.</p><p>One tactic to get more juice out of your blog posts is to offer placement to your partners. That way, a single blog post doesn&apos;t just earn you one backlink, but also builds reciprocity with your network of fellow link builders.</p><p>Begin with five strong partners and expand slowly.<strong> That&apos;s not a shortcut</strong>&#x2014;finding five people who reliably collaborate takes real effort. But five active partners will outperform fifty who joined a Slack channel and never logged in again.</p><p>To start these conversations, browse our <a href="https://hunter.io/templates/marketing/partnership"><u>partnership email templates</u></a>.</p><h2 id="link-building-strategies-worth-your-time-ranked"><strong>Link building strategies worth your time (ranked)</strong></h2><p>There are many ways to obtain links for your website.</p><p>The basic premise is:</p><ol><li>You find a website that matches your criteria (topical relevance to your business, healthy traffic, and link profile).</li><li>You identify the best person to contact (someone who can directly manage content on the website).</li><li>You reach out via email&#x2014;and sometimes add more channels to the mix.</li></ol><p>But there are many ways to find relevant websites, and many strategies to leverage.</p><p>Not all of these strategies deserve equal effort. Here&apos;s how I rank them by impact, based on years of running outreach at scale.</p><h3 id="editorial-links-from-high-quality-websites-link-insertions"><strong>Editorial links from high-quality websites (link insertions)</strong></h3><p>This is the holy grail of link building, and it&apos;s the strategy I prioritize above everything else.</p><p>An editorial link means getting your link inserted into an article. But not just any article.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img alt="Link Building Outreach: A Complete Guide" class="kg-image" height="1078" src="https://hunter.io/blog/content/images/2026/04/image-2.png" width="2000" /></figure><p>What&apos;s key is that you find websites highly relevant to your own website. So if you&apos;re building links for a CRM product, you need other websites that talk about sales, recruitment, customer management&#x2014;not Christmas decorations or Formula 1.</p><p>Then, in the ideal scenario, the websites you target should also:</p><ul><li>Have a healthy link profile (avoid websites that are overused for link building&#x2014;a telltale sign is pages with dozens, if not hundreds, of editorial links),</li><li>Already have some traffic from Google, and</li><li>Have a high Domain Rating.</li></ul><div class="kg-card kg-callout-card kg-callout-card-blue"><div class="kg-callout-emoji">&#x1f4a1;</div><div class="kg-callout-text">The website with way more outgoing links than incoming links is a suspect link farm. Everything with a ratio of 1:1 or higher should be investigated. If you see a 2:1 ratio (outgoing:incoming), in 99% of cases, it is a link farm.</div></div><p>A single editorial link from a relevant, healthy website can be worth 10+ regular backlinks, because what matters isn&apos;t just the domain&#x2014;it&apos;s the specific page. A link from a DR 60 article that ranks on page one for a competitive term and gets thousands of monthly visitors sends far more value than a link from a high-DR domain&apos;s blog post that nobody reads.</p><p>Editorial link building is also scalable. Once you find articles where your product or content fits naturally, the ask is straightforward.</p><h3 id="listicle-outreach"><strong>Listicle outreach</strong></h3><p>Listicle outreach targets listicles where your product or company could appear. The goal is to secure placement and fair coverage in a list of competing solutions.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img alt="Link Building Outreach: A Complete Guide" class="kg-image" height="1509" src="https://hunter.io/blog/content/images/2026/04/image.png" width="2000" /></figure><p>Listicle placements serve double duty in 2026: they help with traditional SERP rankings and with LLM visibility. If your product appears in &quot;best X tools&quot; or &quot;top Y solutions&quot; articles, AI models that crawl and cite these pages may also reference your brand.</p><p>A key advantage of achieving placements in listicles is the ability to influence the narrative. Typically, when an editor agrees to feature your product, they will request details about its capabilities, pricing, plans, and other information relevant to readers.&#xa0;</p><p>While this doesn&apos;t allow for exaggeration or misleading claims (editors usually ensure a neutral presentation), it does provide a valuable chance to accurately describe your features, including the promotion of any new ones.&#xa0;</p><p>This control is vital because when others, especially competitors, place your product in their listicles, they often actually mislead their readers to downplay your strengths, or even openly lie about your pricing or lacking features.</p><div class="kg-card kg-callout-card kg-callout-card-blue"><div class="kg-callout-emoji">&#x1f4a1;</div><div class="kg-callout-text">For us, this has been very concrete. Early listicles positioned Hunter as an email finding and verification service. As we evolved into an all-in-one email outreach platform, we updated the write-ups to reflect that. Same placements, new messaging.</div></div><p>Listicles also capture bottom-of-funnel traffic. Visitors reading &quot;best email outreach tools&quot; are comparing options and may convert. That makes listicle backlinks valuable beyond just their SEO impact.</p><h3 id="guest-post-outreach"><strong>Guest post outreach</strong></h3><p>Guest posting is when you reach out to websites with an article pitch. You offer free content&#x2013;they offer placement for your link.</p><p>Guest posting takes more effort than the other strategies: you need a topic pitch, an outline, a finished draft, and patience while the editor reviews and publishes. Communication delays are real&#x2014;a single guest post can take weeks from pitch to publication.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img alt="Link Building Outreach: A Complete Guide" class="kg-image" height="906" src="https://hunter.io/blog/content/images/2026/04/image-1.png" width="1412" /></figure><p>But it&apos;s a must-have. Guest posts establish you as an authority in your niche, and each one creates two to four link building opportunities. You can include your own backlink, add links for partners (which strengthens your relationships), and use the published piece as a sample when pitching higher-authority sites.</p><p>The progression looks like this: </p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img alt="Link Building Outreach: A Complete Guide" class="kg-image" height="1080" src="https://hunter.io/blog/content/images/2026/04/image-4.png" width="1440" /></figure><p>I treat guest posting as link building maintenance work. It&apos;s not the most effective at face value, but it helps you build long-term relationships and topical authority.</p><p>For pitch templates, see our<a href="https://hunter.io/templates/seo/guest-post"> <u>guest post email templates</u></a>.</p><h3 id="unlinked-brand-mentions"><strong>Unlinked brand mentions</strong></h3><p>Unlinked mentions are when your brand or product is covered on other websites, but without an actual &lt;a href&gt; link.</p><p>If someone already mentions your brand in their content without linking to you, asking for a link is one of the easiest outreach wins.</p><p>But... I don&apos;t prioritize it.</p><p>An unlinked brand mention is already powerful for brand signals&#x2014;adding a link is incrementally better, not dramatically better.</p><p>Pursue these opportunistically, not as a core strategy. We run recurring campaigns (think twice a year) to cover all unlinked mentions in regular sweeps.</p><h3 id="what-we-skip-and-why"><strong>What we skip (and why)</strong></h3><p><strong>Broken link building.</strong> In theory, you find broken links on relevant pages and offer your content as a replacement. In practice, any website worth getting a link from is already monitoring and fixing their broken links. The yield is too low to justify the prospecting time. If you really want to pursue this strategy, try to cover as many broken links as possible with one recurring campaign and move on to other methods.</p><p><strong>The skyscraper technique.</strong> The premise: you find an article that could naturally mention your product, and you reach out with a suggestion that your link can make the current article better. This worked years ago. Today, it&apos;s overused and underperforming. Most people I know in the space have moved on.</p><p>I&apos;d rather be honest about what doesn&apos;t work than pad this guide with strategies that sound good but waste your time.</p><h2 id="how-to-find-the-right-websites"><strong>How to find the right websites</strong></h2><h3 id="using-ahrefs-content-explorer"><strong>Using Ahrefs Content Explorer</strong></h3><p>Ahrefs is my primary prospecting tool because of its filtering capabilities.
